GOALS, OBJECTIVES, AND POLICIES
This section stipulates goals, objectives, and implementing policies for the Intergovernmental
Coordination Element pursuant to §163.3177(6)(h), Florida Statutes.

GOAL 7.1 PROVIDE MECHANISMS                         FOR     IMPROVED INTERGOVERNMENTAL
COORDINATION
The City shall undertake actions necessary to establish governmental relationships designed to maintain
and improve the coordination of public and private entities involved in development activities, growth
management, and resource conservation.

       OBJECTIVE 7.1.1 PROCESS FOR INTERGOVERNMENTAL COORDINATION.
       Systematically coordinate the development and implementation of the City's Comprehensive Plan
       with the plans and programs of Osceola County, the School District of Osceola County,
       MetroPlan Orlando, LYNX, and other federal, state, regional, local, and non-governmental entities
       through formal and informal means.

               POLICY 7.1.1.1: RESPONSIBLE ENTITY FOR INTERGOVERNMENTAL COORDINATION.
               The City Commission shall continue to be responsible for ensuring an effective
               intergovernmental coordination program for the City, including maintenance of a
               Comprehensive Plan consistent with the State Comprehensive Plan and East Central
               Florida Regional Planning Council Regional Policy Plan. The City Manager or his
               designated representative shall represent the City in coordinating matters of common
               interest on technical coordination committees. These forums shall be used to prevent or
               eliminate duplication of governmental activities.

               POLICY 7.1.1.2: COORDINATION WITH ADJACENT JURISDICTIONS.
               The City will continue to assist Osceola County and Orange County with coordinated
               planning for unincorporated areas within one and one-half miles of the City limits. The
               City shall consider compatibility with adopted land use plans of adjacent local governments
               and will request copies of proposed amendments within this area for the City's review to
               promote consistency with the City's adopted Plan.

               POLICY 7.1.1.3: COORDINATION OF GROWTH MANAGEMENT ISSUES.
               The City shall continue to pursue resolution of growth management issues with impacts
               transcending the City's political jurisdiction, by participating on ad hoc technical
               coordination committees, through joint technical analysis of mutual problems and issues,
               and through structures for conflict resolution that are designed to further the goals,

               The City and Osceola County shall work to establish an informal mediation process for
               solving intergovernmental coordination problems in a timely manner among local
               governments and other units of local governments providing services, or those entities
               without regulatory authority over the use of land.

       The City will consider entering into a Joint Planning Agreement (JPA) with Osceola County to

               contain specific criteria for the annexation of land. Criteria could include but not be limited

               recreation, and public safety facilities, and other issues determined to be important within

               review of proposed future land use designation amendments.

               opportunity to review development proposals within the JPA with regard to comprehensive

DATA, INVENTORY, AND ANALYSIS
INTRODUCTION
In order to successfully implement the City’s vision, a host of governmental and non-governmental
entities must coordinate their activities and work together for the common good. This chapter describes
the relationships the City has with other entities and organizations, and identifies planning agreements
and initiatives the City is engaged in to provide services and maintain a safe and healthy local
environment.

EXISTING INTERGOVERNMENTAL COORDINATION
This section provides an inventory of governmental entities and agencies with formal or informal
relationships with the City of Kissimmee.

LOCAL AND REGIONAL ENTITIES
Coordination among entities whose primary focus is local or regional is essential for the continuation and
implementation of the City’s vision and adopted goals. While regional entities provide a means for
greater interaction and cooperation throughout the Orlando Metro Area, special districts and local
entities, especially Osceola County, have more direct and immediate interactions with the City on a daily
basis. This close interaction allows, among other things, for the reduction of potential conflicts in the
continuous provision of public services.

ADJACENT LOCAL GOVERNMENTS
The City of Kissimmee is surrounded by unincorporated
portions of Osceola County to the north, east, southwest,
and west. Lake Tohopekaliga lies to the City's immediate
south. The City coordinates its growth management
program with Osceola County and actively participates
on various ad hoc Osceola County advisory groups to the
County Commission.

The City has coordinated a number of major issues with the County, primarily through interlocal
agreements (ILAs) and informal administrative functions. These coordination items include annexations,
transportation improvements, fire protection services and emergency management response, and
recreation services. The City and the County have also worked closely for the passage of the additional
one and a half cent sales tax to increase local revenues, as well as legislative issues, economic
development and housing initiatives, and for the protection of natural resources along Shingle Creek with
the Shingle Creek Regional Park.

SPECIAL DISTRICTS
The Community Redevelopment Agency (CRA) is one of two special taxing districts located within the
corporate limits of the City of Kissimmee. There are two Community Redevelopment Areas (CRAs) in
the City:

   •     The Downtown Community CRA is in the downtown area of Kissimmee generally from the
         lakefront area north to US 192/Vine Street, and from John Young Parkway east to the CSX
         railroad tracks.

   •     The Vine Street CRA is located along the Vine Street corridor approximately a ¼-mile north and
         ¼-mile south of the corridor for the entire city limits.

The CRA uses tax increment financing as a major source of funding for various capital projects and
economic development efforts for Vine Street and the downtown’s business and residential districts.
Other funding mechanisms the CRA uses include grants and local option sales tax collections.

The CRA is headed by a manager employed by the City and the City Commission serves as the advisory
board that meets monthly regarding CRA activities and plans.

SCHOOL DISTRICT OF OSCEOLA COUNTY
The Osceola County School Board is authorized through statutes enacted by the Florida Legislature, and
residents of the City help fund public school operations through an ad valorem tax assessment. The City,
other local governments in the County, coordinate with the School District regarding school locations and
the planning of school sites and the facilities needed to support them.

The School Board and the City signed an interlocal agreement in October 1998 that promotes
coordination of school site development from the long-range planning stage through the site layout
stage. Among the topics addressed in that agreement are: (1) coordination of planning for residential
development; (2) coordination and selection of school locations;(3) coordination of planning in acquisition
of schools; (4) purchasing and leasing of educational facilities other than schools; (5) comprehensive
planning and zoning for educational facilities; (6) coordination of site planning for educational facilities;
(7) review and processing costs; (8) compliance with State laws; (9) off-site improvements for expanded
sites; and (10) joint-use of facilities and sharing of resources.

An updated agreement signed in July 2009 recognizes the need for coordination of residential
development activity and school facilities planning. This interlocal agreement (ILA) also references the

TOHOPEKALIGA WATER AUTHORITY
Established in October 2003 by a special act of the Florida legislature, the Tohopekaliga Water Authority
(TWA) is the largest provider of water, wastewater, and reclaimed water services in Osceola County. The
Authority was established for the sole purpose of providing regional stewardship over water resources in
Osceola County. TWA took over all water, wastewater, and reclaimed water services originally provided
by the City to its residents.

The TWA is governed by a six-member board of supervisors responsible for approving all operating
policies and the nearly $124 million operating budget. The City appoints one member to the board of
supervisors.

SOUTH FLORIDA WATER MANAGEMENT DISTRICT
South Florida Water Management District (SFWMD), created by Chapter 25270 Laws of Florida (1949),
is a multi-county independent special district responsible for flood control and water conservation in the
South Florida region. The SFWMD is divided into five water supply planning areas, with the City located
in the Upper Kissimmee Basin near the District’s northernmost boundary in Orange County.

The SFWMD has regulatory authority over the use of land and may require administrative review and
permitting of development proposals pursuant to state statute and the Florida Administrative Code. A
development proposal may be required to obtain a management and storage of surface water permit, or
construction permit pursuant to relevant State statutes and codes. The SFWMD Department of Resource
Management has primary responsibility for coordination of these permitting procedures, and the City’s
Community Development Director (or designee) remains the principal liaison with the SFWMD.

The District is also responsible for coordinating with the City regarding water supply planning. In the
City’s case, since TWA is the water supplier, the City must coordinate with the District and TWA
regarding maintenance of adequate water supplies through the planning period. The District also

EAST CENTRAL FLORIDA REGIONAL PLANNING COUNCIL
The East Central Florida Regional Planning Council (ECFRPC) is a public planning agency that serves
Brevard, Lake, Marion, Orange, Osceola, Seminole, Sumter, and Volusia counties and their cities. The
ECFRPC advocates for cooperative planning efforts at the regional and sub-regional levels, and to
otherwise bring together the public and private sectors to resolve issues of regional interest. The
activities of the ECFRPC are overseen by a 32 member board of local elected officials and Governor
appointed citizens.

The ECFRPC's current work program includes specific regional planning initiatives including the efforts
associated with the MyRegion.org regional visioning activity, which provided development scenarios of
the Orlando Metro Area through 2060. The ECFRPC also coordinates for emergency and hurricane
response planning, management of the Lake Apopka and Wekiva River basins, and for lake protection.
Other ongoing regional programs include an affordable housing information clearinghouse, power plants
and other types of regional-scale facilities.

The ECFRPC is assisting the City and the County
regarding the locations and designations of greenways
and trails and the development of a county-wide trails
system. The City Community Development Director, or
their designee, is the City's principal liaison on
technical issues involving the City's Comprehensive
Plan and regional planning activities. The Planning
Advisory Board and the City Commission maintain
responsibility for managing policy issues surrounding
regional planning activities.

METROPLAN ORLANDO
MetroPlan Orlando is the region's Metropolitan Planning Organization (MPO). MetroPlan is responsible
for comprehensive transportation planning throughout the Orlando metropolitan area, and is comprised
of elected officials from local governments located within that area. The City of Kissimmee is represented
on the MetroPlan board by one of its elected officials, as appointed by the City Commission. Through
comprehensive transportation planning procedures and processes, MetroPlan develops the Long Range
Transportation Plan, from which a Transportation Improvement Program is developed and adopted.
MetroPlan also considers bicycle, pedestrian, mass transportation, and airport facilities in its planning
efforts.

CENTRAL FLORIDA REGIONAL TRANSPORTATION AUTHORITY
The Central Florida Regional Transportation Authority, or LYNX, provides mass transit services in the
City and within the greater Orlando Metro area. The City shares a portion of its gas tax revenue with
LYNX in return for transit services for residents and visitors. The City coordinates with LYNX regarding
various mass transit issues and the development of new routes within the City.

Commuter rail is planned to be provided to Kissimmee in the future and the City coordinates with
various transportation and agencies for the stations proposed Downtown and near Poinciana. The
Central Florida Commuter Rail Commission (CFCRC) oversee SunRail and consists of
representatives from the five funding agencies.

KISSIMMEE UTILITY AUTHORITY
The Kissimmee Utility Authority (KUA) continues to provide electrical power service to area residents,
and the City’s power system is connected to the statewide electric grid. In the late 1990's KUA separated
from the City's pension plan, and other financial ties are limited, in recognition of the changing climate for
electrical service providers. KUA is still under control of the City Commission, in that it is run by a Board
appointed by the City Commission. KUA continues to annually submit a capital and operating budget to
the City Commission.

SOLID WASTE SERVICES
Although the City provides residential solid waste collection services, it maintains franchise agreements
for commercial solid waste collection services within the City. Wastes collected in the City are deposited
at the J.E.D. Solid Waste Management Facility, operated by Omni Waste of Osceola County. This
private landfill facility in Holopaw is also the deposition site for wastes collected throughout Osceola
County. The City, the County, and the City of St. Cloud maintain an ILA for the continued deposition of
wastes at this facility.

NATURAL GAS SERVICES
The City of Kissimmee maintains a franchise agreement with Peoples Gas, a subsidiary of TECO Energy
Incorporated, to supply residential, commercial, and industrial natural gas demands within its corporate
limits.

FLORIDA DEPARTMENT OF ECONOMIC OPPORTUNITY
As the State Land Planning Agency, the Florida Department of Economic Opportunity (DEO) provides
technical assistance to local governments with respect to the following service areas: 1) rural and urban
small business assistance and economic development; 2) community planning; 3) assistance programs
for low-income Floridians; and 4) special district information. DEO administers a variety of grant
programs including those focused on infrastructure, economic development, housing rehabilitation, and
revitalization.

The City of Kissimmee coordinates with DEO in maintaining the adopted Comprehensive Plan, pursuant
to Chapter 163, F.S. The City Community Development Director, or designee, serves as principal liaison
on comprehensive plan activities, including typical amendments to the plan, Evaluation and Appraisal
Reports (EAR), and EAR-based Plan amendments.

FLORIDA DEPARTMENT OF ENVIRONMENTAL PROTECTION
The Florida Department of Environmental Protection (DEP) is among the most diverse state agencies,
and is responsible for regulatory programs, land and recreation, and planning and management.

The DEP seeks to preserve the quality of State land, water, and air resources through the regulation of
industrial and household wastes, hazardous wastes, air pollution emission, solid waste disposal, dredge
and fill activities, water quality and quantity, and alterations to wetlands and other environmentally
sensitive areas.

The DEP is also the State permitting agency having environmental regulatory authority over the use of
land within waters of the State. Construction activities, including dock and pier construction, involving

FLORIDA DEPARTMENT OF TRANSPORTATION
The Florida Department of Transportation (FDOT) directs transportation planning functions and project

construction, maintenance, and related activities of the Florida Highway System, and in particular the
Strategic Intermodal System (SIS). The SIS includes transportation facilities that are of particular
importance to the movement of people and goods within the State.

The FDOT maintains a measure of regulatory authority over the use of lands along State roads, including
setting design standards for curb cuts on the State's highway system. In addition, FDOT is responsible
for determining the functional classification of state roads within the City. The City’s Public Works and
Engineering Director, or designee, serves as principal liaison with FDOT for roadways, the airport, and
rail facilities.

FLORIDA DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH
Florida's Department of Health (DOH) is responsible for the health and safety of all citizens and visitors
to the state. As a public health agency, the Department is charged with monitoring the health status of
Floridians, diagnosing and investigating health problems and mobilizing local communities to address
health-related issues. The Department develops policies and plans that support public health goals,
enforces laws and regulations that protect the public's health, links people to needed health care
services and provides services where necessary to assure access. The Department also provides
specialized assistance to pregnant women, infants and children with special health care needs, and
licenses and regulates health care practitioners. The City principally coordinates through the regional
office and through the County Health Department, which incorporates not only conventional public health
functions but also environmental health. DOH is charged with managing a broad range of environmental
issues, including septic tank regulation, water quality, and pollution control.

FLORIDA DEPARTMENT OF CHILDREN AND FAMILIES
The Department of Children and Families (DCF) is unique among state agencies because it is composed
of locally-designed and operated entities that meet the special needs of the communities they serve. The
City principally coordinates through the Circuit 9 of the Central Florida regional office of DCF. District 9
includes Orange and Osceola counties. The City coordinates with DCF on issues surrounding delivery of
rehabilitative social and medical services for children, youth, family, and elderly, including services
directed toward special needs.

FLORIDA FISH AND WILDLIFE CONSERVATION COMMISSION
The Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission (FFWCC) monitors water conditions and wildlife
associated with Lake Tohopekaliga, Shingle Creek, and Mill Slough. The Commission continues to
monitor water quality and fish populations in this important waterbody. The Commission also reviews
projects that may have potential negative impacts on local fish and wildlife habitats, or that may intrude
and disturb habitats occupied by listed species.

FLORIDA DEPARTMENT OF STATE
The City of Kissimmee coordinates with the Florida Department of State primarily on issues related to
State archives and records, historic sites and properties, libraries, and fine arts. The City works with the
Division of Historical Resources in addressing comprehensive planning issues involving historical and
archaeological sites of significance. The Department of State is also a City source for obtaining rules and
regulations promulgated by State agencies. Similarly, the City coordinates with the Department of State
in carrying out regulatory procedures of State administrative law.

FLORIDA DEPARTMENT OF AGRICULTURE AND CONSUMER SERVICES
The City of Kissimmee coordinates with the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services
(DOACS), Division of Forestry, as well as its Agriculture Agent Extension Service, both of which maintain
personnel within Osceola County. The City staff and residents receive technical assistance, consumer
related services, and publications that address a broad range of special services provided by DOACS.
The Division of Forestry manages the growth and preservation of State woodlands within Osceola
County, as well as authorizes controlled burning of grasslands, and flatwood understories. The extension
office has also assisted local governments in the promotion of eco-tourism and the development of the
county-wide ad hoc greenways and trails sub-committee.

INTERLOCAL AGREEMENTS
A principal method by which governmental entities engage in enforceable agreements is the interlocal
agreement (ILA). The City maintains agreements with entities as varied as special districts and Charter
counties, and enters into these agreements as formally executed legal documents binding each of the
signing parties to a set of agreed upon actions.

As noted above, the City maintains an ILA with the Tohopekaliga Water Authority for the provision of
water and wastewater services, as well as several ILAs with the School Board and the other local
governments in Osceola County for public school planning. The City of Kissimmee and Osceola County
have an ILA that addresses the provision of fire rescue services, whereby each agency will cover areas
closest to their facilities regardless of jurisdictional boundaries in order to maintain the health, safety, and
welfare of residents. Additionally, the City and County maintain an ILA to jointly manage lands
purchased for resource management. The Shingle Creek Regional Park is among these jointly
maintained areas, and both entities work cooperatively to implement the park’s management plan.

The City of Kissimmee and Osceola County also
maintain an ILA with a private company regarding
operation and maintenance of the solid waste
collection and disposal system. Under the terms
of the agreement, the City maintains responsibility
for collecting solid waste within its incorporated
area and uses the County’s transfer sites prior to
deposition at the privately owned landfill site in
Holopaw.

The City and County also maintain ILAs regarding
the collection and distribution of development
impact and mobility fees and the county's Public Service Taxes. As a result, the City agrees to charge
County residents the same amount as City residents for the use of City recreational facilities, and the
City provides a portion of transportation fees to the County for road maintenance and construction.

The City of Kissimmee and Osceola County entered into an ILA authorizing the County to develop a local
hazard mitigation plan for the county. The county developed a single, unified local mitigation strategy to
pre-identify and prioritize hazard mitigation projects in unincorporated and municipal local government
jurisdictions. The City cooperated with the County in developing the Local Mitigation Strategy (LMS), and
DEO was also instrumental in development of the LMS, which has in turn become part of the Statewide
Hazard Mitigation Strategy.

JOINT PLANNING INITIATIVES
The City of Kissimmee actively supports the ECFRPC, including through the participation of elected
officials and staff on boards and committees. City staff assists the ECFRPC, and also the County and the
School Board, in coordinating land use, population, public school student population, and housing data.

The City also actively supports MetroPlan Orlando, the region's Metropolitan Planning Organization
(MPO). One of the City’s commissioners serves on the MetroPlan Orlando Board, while staff members
represent the City on several MetroPlan committees and sub-committees. These committees and
boards address various transportation needs throughout the region. In addition to MetroPlan, the City
also supports LYNX, the Central Florida Regional Transportation Authority. LYNX provides bus service to
the area. The City shares a portion of its gas tax revenue with LYNX in return for bus service. The City
coordinates with LYNX regarding various mass transit issues and the development of new routes within
the City. Through transportation related the committees, the City also coordinates with the Central
Florida Commuter Rail Commission responsible for SunRail funding.

The City of Kissimmee has a formal agreement with the South Florida Water Management District
(SFWMD) whereby the SFWMD reviews all site plans within the City of Kissimmee. The primary focuses
of the SFWMD reviews are to:

   Determine and enforce acceptable surface water management standards;

   Assure enforcement of applicable water quantity and quality standards;

   Assist in floodplain management; and

   Assure preservation of wetlands.

DISPUTE RESOLUTION
If the City and another governmental entity or agency cannot come to agreement on an issue of concern,
the City has determined that the ECFRPC is the body through which intergovernmental disagreements
are to be settled – principally through mediation – unless another entity is agreed upon by both parties to
the dispute. If such disputes cannot be resolved through mediated discussions, or other techniques
used by the regional planning council, the City could take more formal judicial actions, but such steps are
costly in time and resources.

ADMINISTRATIVE-LEVEL COORDINATION ACTIVITIES
Osceola County assists the City of Kissimmee in enforcing the Federal Aviation Administration height
regulations, including locally adopted airport approach zone regulations, by reviewing all site plans within
unincorporated areas situated within the boundaries of the airport approach zones. This review process
assists in preventing hazards to air navigation which otherwise might occur without more prudent
management. The City and County also reciprocate in reviewing site plans adjacent to respective
common boundaries to determine whether the developments will have negative impacts on their
respective public facilities.

The City of Kissimmee and the Osceola County School Board maintain site plan review coordination, and
to maintain compliance with the City’s Land Development Code and State law. In addition, staff from the
School Board participate on the City's Development Review Committee to determine whether a
development proposal will produce traffic impacts, increased student enrollments, or otherwise impact
school facilities in the Concurrency Service Area of a proposed development.

ENHANCING INTERGOVERNMENTAL COORDINATION
The City of Kissimmee has identified several intergovernmental issues that may require additional or
heightened levels of coordination. The City’s vision of an interconnected multimodal transportation
system relies heavily on continued cooperation and communication between LYNX, Osceola County,
MetroPlan, and the FDOT. These coordination activities may be specifically determined in the future and
included as policies in the Comprehensive Plan, or they may be included in a formal ILA.

The City's involvement with the County and other regulatory agencies, with respect to addressing other
issues that may arise, are ongoing. The City of Kissimmee will continue to coordinate with the entities
and agencies listed in this Element regarding multi-jurisdictional issues involving growth and land
development, the supply and provision of public facilities and services, and resource conservation. Each
Element of this Comprehensive Plan describes proactive approaches for maintaining and refining
coordination activities and processes that may provide more effective services or management,
depending on the situation. In order to promote and further the regional and local visions, these
approaches will be applied to solve coordination issues and/or conflicts, as appropriate.
