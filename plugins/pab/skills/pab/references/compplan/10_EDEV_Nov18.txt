GOALS, OBJECTIVES, AND POLICIES
This section stipulates goals, objectives, and implementing policies for this optional local government
comprehensive plan element, pursuant to §163.3177(7)(j), Florida Statutes.

GOAL 10.1: IMPLEMENT ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT RESOURCES.
Develop strategies to implement economic development activities within the City.

       OBJECTIVE 10.1.1: ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT DIVISION.
       Maintain the City’s economic development division, which works with local and regional
       organizations to promote business recruitment and retention in the City.

               POLICY 10.1.1.1: ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT FUND.
               Refine funding mechanism and establish an Economic Development Fund Policy, to
               advance the interests of retaining and recruiting businesses or targeted industries.

               POLICY 10.1.1.2: ECONOMIC INCENTIVES.
               Create and maintain guidelines for development and redevelopment incentives, which are
               flexible and can be modified as the economy dictates, to retain and recruit businesses and
               targeted industries.

               POLICY 10.1.1.3: ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT MARKETING.
               Publicize, promote, and market the City’s assets, as well as the beneficial impacts of
               business investments within the City.

               POLICY 10.1.1.4: BUSINESS RETENTION AND EXPANSION.
               Promote retention and expansion of existing businesses.

GOAL 10.2: INCREASE ECONOMIC OPPORTUNITIES.
Cultivate Kissimmee’s economy to provide more workforce opportunities for residents, sustainable
services to the local market, and stable revenue sources for local businesses

       OBJECTIVE 10.2.1: DIVERSIFY KISSIMMEE ECONOMY.
       Target market sectors that are currently underrepresented in the Kissimmee economy.

               POLICY 10.2.1.1: TARGET INDUSTRIES AND BUSINESSES.
               Maintain and periodically revise a list of industries and businesses to be targeted for
               participation in the local business market.

DATA, INVENTORY, AND ANALYSIS
INTRODUCTION
As noted in the 2016 Evaluation and Appraisal Report, the key overarching issue for the City of
Kissimmee is economic development. This issue is attached to attracting and retaining diverse and
quality employment opportunities, improving quality of life, and continued growth to be competitive in the
Central Florida region and beyond. A major focus is on providing more jobs in general, and higher paying
jobs in particular. Of particular interest are industries related to medical, technical, and aviation-related
businesses.

For years, the City’s economy has relied on tourism and associated services, with their preponderance of
relatively low-wage jobs. However, the City also has robust employment in the public and medical
sectors, with technology companies forming some of the most recent new additions to employment in
and near the City.

EXISTING CONDITIONS
This section profiles the income and employment statistics for City residents and local businesses.

keep pace with the income levels of local jurisdictions. Table 10-1 below shows the median income
levels for various geographies and the table focus is on relevant information regarding City residents’

The data shows that despite low unemployment rates and the lower wages typically found in this
industry, the employment share in the Leisure and Hospitality industry continues to grow. This industry
has held a steady share of the job market at 21.5 percent or higher since 2014, or over one fifth of total
employment, despite an increase in the Construction industry which has historically been inversely
related to the Leisure and Hospitality industry. While the Construction industry employment is similar to
that found elsewhere in the State, the Orlando-Kissimmee MSA has a significantly higher reliance on the
Leisure and Hospitality industry.

In contrast, the Educational and Health Trade, Transportation and Utilities, and Professional and
Business Services industries maintained relatively steady employment percentages over time. The
exception is a slight decrease in the Educational and Health industry from 18.2 percent in 2010 to 17.9
percent in 2016. In these industries the MSA has tended to maintain a lower percentage of employment
than the state, except for Professional and Business Services, which is likely due to downtown Orlando’s
status as a location for business headquarters. The proportional differences between the MSA and
Florida may be partially attributed to the higher percentage of MSA employment in the Leisure and
Hospitality industry. A reduction in employment in the variable Leisure and Hospitality industry, and a
corresponding increase in the more stable Educational and Health, Trade, Transportation and Utilities,
and Professional and Business Services industries, could provide the MSA (and more specifically the
City of Kissimmee) with the potential to improve its employment diversity and economic base.

In addition to numerous small, home-grown businesses, and mid-size regional and national chains, the
City is home to several large, private employers, among which all three are within the Educational and

ECONOMIC POTENTIAL
In many cases, investment by outside entities first requires local investment. The City has been
proactively engaged in improving economic conditions citywide for several years, with specific emphasis
in several key locations. Among these are the downtown Community Redevelopment Area, the
Kissimmee Gateway Airport area, and along the Vine Street corridor. This section details targeted efforts
within the City.

the deteriorating physical and economic condition of the downtown area. CRA planning efforts aim to
promote infill redevelopment on vacant parcels, which are guided by a set of design criteria adopted in

MEDICAL AND HEALTHCARE
The Osceola Regional Medical Center is located within the City’s Community Redevelopment Area
(CRA). Coordination of business development initiatives with the goals established by the CRA is
essential. The CRA is guided by a Plan and contains a special Tax Increment Financing (TIF) district.
The revenues received from the TIF districts have been used to stimulate redevelopment by improving
the public infrastructure, reducing development impediments, and providing various financial incentives
for business start-up and expansion, in addition to other available incentives, discussed in-depth in the
next section.

Another hospital, Florida Hospital Kissimmee, is located along Main Street/Orange Blossom Trail, north
of both the CRA and the Vine Street redevelopment area. Though not eligible for bonuses associated
with location in either of those two areas, other incentive opportunities are available through the creation
of the Medical Arts District. The location of this District is shown on Future Land Use Element Figure 1-3.
The City has established a policy for businesses growing and locating here, and evaluation or monitoring
of the program in the future may help determine if adjustments are needed.

The goal of this District is to attract a hub of health-care related companies and physician practices
between Florida Hospital Kissimmee and Osceola Regional Medical Center. Designated in April 2016
and offered for three years, new economic incentives created as part of this include:

TRANSPORTATION
In addition to healthcare and general high-tech industries, the Kissimmee Gateway Airport, as a general
aviation facility and reliever to the Orlando International Airport, has the potential to anchor aviation-
related businesses proximal to the airport. In the last five years the City’s economic development efforts
have included attracting aviation academic companies to grow the local workforce skillset. The airport
now has tenants with certification programs in the fields of Flight training, Avionics, and Airframe &
Powerplant (A&P). It’s rare to find all three of these programs at one airport. In addition to growing an
aviation workforce, the City has launched an Aerospace Advancement Initiative at Kissimmee Gateway
Airport. Similar to the Kissimmee Medical Arts District, this initiative offers eligible aerospace companies
economic development incentives to help them offset some of their costs in establishing their company

RESOURCE PROTECTION
Another way to broaden the City’s economy involves highlighting the City’s location and natural
amenities through historical tourism and ecotourism. This includes exploring opportunities to showcase
the City’s wealth of water-based natural resources, and historic sites and character. The water features
and resources in the City already provide prime locations to engage in recreational activities.
Understanding that economic development and protection of important waterways are not mutually
exclusive, the City of Kissimmee can support both initiatives by promoting ecotourism and continuing to
take advantage of public-private partnerships, grant funding, second party acquisitions, and County land
acquisition programs. The Open Space and Recreation map displays the locations of multi-use trails,
parks, and open space areas that could be leveraged to encourage environmental visitors to the City.

Another potential economic growth area is that of historically-oriented tourism. Visitors are drawn to an
area’s history, and the City has a long and colorful one which it should continue to promote via the

ECONOMIC INCENTIVES
In order to attract outside investment, local
governments must often provide incentives to
interested parties. These attractions usually take
the form of tax breaks and other monetary savings.
The section below describes some incentives
available to potential businesses.

FEDERAL AND STATE
One tool the City has leveraged is the availability of
federal funds for redevelopment efforts. The City has previously applied for EPA Brownfield Assessment
Grants in order to fund the assessment of approximately 20 potentially contaminated sites. Once the
contamination level is determined, cleanup efforts can be pursued. The City hopes to utilize this federal
program to spur redevelopment of sites that are currently underutilized due to the environmental stigma
associated with particular properties. One success within Kissimmee through this program is the Lake
Toho Redevelopment. This 10-acre urban renewal project is a transit-oriented development which will
allow for the reuse of two brownfield sites that previously functioned as a power generation plant and a
railroad spur and industrial site.

The State of Florida also offers incentives in the form of tax refunds and exemptions. The Qualified
Target Industry (QTI) Tax Refund provides $3,000 for each new job created in targeted high value-added
industries. To qualify as a QTI, a business must create 10 or more jobs, pay more than 115 percent of
area wages, have local support and have a significant positive impact on the community. The target
industries are defined by the state, and include only those businesses serving multi-state and/or
international markets. Retail activities, and activities regulated by the Division of Hotels and Restaurants
or the Department of Business and Professional Regulation are statutorily excluded from consideration.

The state also maintains a grant program, the Economic Development Transportation Fund, which
provides up to $3 million to local governments for the construction or improvement of transportation
infrastructure needed to accommodate new or expanding industries.

The Florida Job Growth Grant Fund is a relatively new economic development program designed to build
on Florida’s success and economic prosperity since 2010. In its first year, the Florida Job Growth Grant
Fund will provide $85 million for public infrastructure and job training projects that support growth and
employment in Florida’s diverse industries. Public infrastructure projects can include transportation and
utilities needed to support economic development. Workforce training projects will provide Floridians with
transferable, sustainable workforce skills applicable to many employers.

COUNTY AND CITY
The County maintains a number of programs available countywide that are designed to assist
businesses with relocation or expansion plans.

The City of Kissimmee also has its own criteria for targeted industry development and eligibility. Similar
to State guidelines, a company must create at least 10 net new full time permanent jobs, or if expanding
a business, increase employment by at least 10% and pay an average annual wage that is 115% higher
than the local county average wage. State and/or County certified QTI projects will be eligible for the
same incentives offered to City Targeted Industry. Companies must be in one of the City’s target industry
categories or be the company’s corporate headquarters.

The City and Osceola County have also instituted a five-day, fast-track permitting program, which is
extended to eligible Economic Development projects. This ensures that new and existing companies
and corporate relocations can occur without delay. In addition, the City of Kissimmee has a completely
electronic permitting system which helps permits to be reviewed faster and reduces costs for the City and
its customers.

SUMMARY
There are substantial challenges ahead regarding economic development. In a down economic market
businesses tend not to expand, often contracting to the detriment of not just employees, but the
community they serve. Strengthening partnerships with local and regional economic development
organizations, such as the Osceola County Economic Development Commission and the Metro Orlando
Economic Development Council, as well as local
educational institutions are essential to attract
targeted industries and to support and nurture
existing local businesses.

The City’s adopted goals, objectives, and policies of
the Economic Development Element will provide the
framework necessary to support economic
investment programs and opportunities needed to
provide the City with an increased commercial tax
base and higher wage employment opportunities.
