# Alex Alemi: voice profile for editors

Drawn from twelve posts on blog.alexalemi.com (2020-2025), listed at the end. Purpose: keep suggested rewrites and insertions in his voice. Quotes are verbatim, including his spelling.

## 1. Rhythm, length, openings, closings

- Sentences alternate between long, comma-chained thinking-out-loud and very short punches. The long ones often run as comma splices; he does not fear them: "Honestly, this feels low, I know that most houses are supposed to have about 1 air change per hour, though here we are discussing specifically CO2 but I would expect the rates to be the same." Then the punch: "So far so good." / "They must." / "That's absurd." / "There is absolutely no way this can happen."
- Paragraphs are 2-5 sentences. One-sentence paragraphs are common and carry the turn: "What if we didn't have to choose a prior directly?" / "Can we do better?" (used three times as a section pivot in one post) / "Unfortunately, physics tends to stretch those dreams thin."
- Openings are one plain sentence, either the thesis or a scene, no throat-clearing: "Sliderules rule. I'm rather enamored with them." / "We recently bought an Airthings so I have been geeking out looking at plots of our air quality." / "Let's tour some fun ways to do mental math to various precisions." / "Years ago I decided that I didn't know how to tie any knots and needed to remedy the situation."
- Closings are either a short imperative invitation ("Try out the quarters thing." / "Give a shot." / "Check it out and let me know what you think." / "Get yourself a piece of rope, and a free half-hour or so") or a one-paragraph zoom-out to the big picture ("Humanity operates on a truly global scale and we now have very direct influences on the chemistry of the planet."). Never a summary of what was covered.

## 2. Register

- First person singular for opinions and confessions ("I think these people are crazy and wrong"), "we" for doing the math together ("Let's establish some baseline numbers"), "you" for the reader's experience ("Quick, what is 4.5 + 8?").
- Contractions throughout (it's, we'd, doesn't, gotta once). Conversational but never sloppy in the argument; sloppy in spelling (occurance, wierdo, illicited), which the editor should fix silently.
- Formality is that of a physicist talking at a whiteboard: "in typical physicist fashion", "pushing some equations around", "back of the envelope".
- Confession is a recurring stance: "If I'm being honest", "if we're being honest" (a tic; appears in most posts), "Honestly,", "I have to admit", "I feel as though" (very frequent hedge; keep it, don't upgrade to "I believe").

## 3. Vocabulary and tics

- Section-opening connectives: "Let's ..." (about 50 uses), "Now," "Notice that", "Unfortunately," "Fortunately," "Granted," "Naturally," "Of course," "Apropos of nothing," "So far so good." "In fact" is rare; "Notice" and "Unfortunately" do that job.
- Rhetorical questions as pivots, often stacked: "What gives? Where does this mapping come from? Why do we need another way to describe probabilities." (note: the last one gets a period, not a question mark).
- Definitions: term in italics on first use, glossed inline or with "i.e.", "aka", "so called": "so called _reference priors_", "The _significand_ is the front part of a number when written in scientific notation". Uses "i.e." and "e.g." freely, mid-sentence.
- Italics for emphasis on one load-bearing word: "_only_ 8,000 times", "_It should never be used to join two ropes_", "wrong _my entire life_", "_uniform_" and "_distinguishable_" repeated as the technical hinge words of a post.
- Parentheticals are frequent and casual: "(for myself as much as anyone else)", "(if it had unencumbered growth)", "(pronounced "tripi")".
- Numbered sidenotes on almost every post, used for citations, small calculations ("You're like a light bulb (2000 kcal/day)"), tangents, and jokes. Jokes are allowed to live entirely in a note.
- Stock phrases: "we all know and love", "as we are well aware", "a whole slew of", "bang-for-your-buck", "the goto answer", "if you stop to think about it".

## 4. Explaining technical material

- Numbers first, then the model. He establishes concrete magnitudes (100 W human, 21 TW civilization) before any equation, and derives famous quantities from scratch rather than citing them ("Instead of looking up the number, let's see if we can estimate it.").
- Toy problems and personal data: coin flips, a disease test, a 4-letter alphabet, his own house's CO2, his son's question about extra electrons.
- Derivations are shown in full but narrated lightly; the prose around them interprets ("In words: ..."; "Notice what just happened.").
- The reader is a peer who knows the basics: "or precisely what you probably thought it should have been anyway if you've heard of Bayesian inference." He says when a post assumes background ("This post will assume some familiarity with physics.") and otherwise never condescends.
- He invents playful notation and units and means it: tau-pi "tripi", curly-bracket decibel units, degrees of belief in degrees, microbels.

## 5. Humor

Dry, deadpan, and mostly delivered as a proposal taken seriously. The joke is usually the last clause of a sentence or a one-line paragraph after a big number. It is never flagged and never extended past a sentence; he moves on. Modes, with examples:

- Absurd proposal advanced with a straight face: "What if we measure degrees of belief in... degrees." / "As a final, only slightly outlandish proposal, I'll suggest that if we wanted a system that was accurate to 25%, we could simply use decibels."
- Mock salesmanship, then a flat non-question: "This mapping has a beautiful mathematical justification, gives rise to beautiful visualizations, beautifully aligns with our existing intuitions and has a beautifully simple approximation. What more could you want."
- Deadpan italic understatement after a huge number: "So it would _only_ take ~30 x 300 ~ 10,000 years." / "Our roughly 50x the total age of the universe!"
- Wordplay presented as accident, confessed in a note: "We may have just gone around in a circle,6" with sidenote "Pun very much intended." / "the most distinguished mathematical measure of distinguishability: the KL divergence."
- Homely scenario inside a formal argument: "if some wierdo walks up to me and asks me to start predicting a whole sequence of coin flips I shouldn't discount the possiblity they are trying to play me for a fool." / "Being good probabilistic thinkers with a penchant for gambling, what we're interested in is:"
- Mock-pedantry, then self-correction: section titled "Obnoxious Precision": "specifying it to a precision of 0.0000005 days or _40 milliseconds_! That's absurd." / "If we wanted to be erroneously pedantic".
- Self-deprecation about his own life: "I had been tying my shoes wrong _my entire life_." / "Since I forgot the hitch I picked last time around, I clearly made a poor choice."
- Pop-culture one-liners, unexplained: "An elegant weapon for a more civilized age." / figure caption "Belief-O-Meter." / a sidenote noting that Ylvis "tied their double shoelace bow in the incorrect Granny Knot orientation".
- Bold opinion stated flatly: "I think these people are crazy and wrong."

What he does NOT do: no emoji, no memes, no sarcasm at the reader, no jokes at named people, no "lol"/"haha", no extended bits or callbacks, no winking "(see what I did there)". Exclamation marks are rare and reserved for a genuinely absurd magnitude ("That's absurd." usually follows). Even then the next sentence returns to work.

## 6. Do / don't for suggested rewrites

Do:
- Open a section with "Let's ..." or a short rhetorical question.
- Land the joke as the final clause and keep it under one sentence; a sidenote is a fine home for it.
- Use "Notice", "Unfortunately", "Granted", "Honestly" as transitions.
- Italicize the single word that carries the surprise ("_only_", "_exactly_").
- Keep his hedges ("I feel as though", "seems to", "something like") and his comma splices when the sentence is thinking aloud.
- Convert claims to numbers; he would rather estimate than assert.
- End with an invitation or a one-paragraph zoom-out, not a recap.

Don't:
- Add "In conclusion", bullet-point summaries, or "Key takeaways".
- Add exclamation marks, emoji, or emphatic adverbs (very/truly are his; "incredibly" is not).
- Explain a joke, extend it, or add a second one in the same paragraph.
- Replace "we" with "one", or "gotta"/"goto" with formal equivalents.
- Introduce jargon without an italicized first use and a plain gloss.
- Cut a personal aside (his house, his son, his shoelaces); those are structural.
